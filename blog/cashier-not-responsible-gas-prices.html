Your Cashier Didn't Raise Gas Prices — Here's Who Actually Did

Published May 15, 2026 · Amoco BP Elburn

If you've ever walked into a gas station, seen the price on the sign, and taken it out on the person behind the counter — this one's for you.

We get it. Prices are high. They've been high for a while. And when you're filling up your tank and watching the numbers climb, it's natural to feel frustrated. But the cashier ringing you up at Amoco BP Elburn had nothing to do with it.

Here's how it actually works.

The Price on the Sign Comes From a Chain We Don't Control

Every morning, BP's wholesale terminal posts a rack price — that's the cost we pay per gallon before we've added a drop of margin. That price is set by global crude oil markets, supply disruptions, refinery capacity, pipeline logistics, and government taxes.

We have no vote in any of it. In 2026, the ongoing conflict near the Strait of Hormuz has been the biggest driver. That waterway handles about 20% of the world's seaborne oil. Every time there's an escalation, crude spikes. Every time there's a peace signal, it drops. We're watching it every single day.

Illinois Taxes Take More Per Gallon Than We Make

Before we earn a single cent of margin, Illinois and the federal government have already collected over $0.98 per gallon in taxes — state motor fuel tax, federal excise tax, Kane County tax, underground storage tank fee, and more.

We're not the government. We can't change that line.

The person at the counter is just doing their job. Our cashiers come in, do their shift, help customers, and go home. They don't set prices. They don't call crude oil traders. They don't negotiate with the state legislature. They just show up.

When prices are high and someone is rude to the person at the register, that's misdirected anger — and it's not fair. If you're frustrated about gas prices, we understand. So are we. But take it up with OPEC, Washington, or Springfield. Not with the person handing you your change.

We Try to Keep Prices Fair

At Amoco BP Elburn, we monitor the wholesale rack every single day. We time our orders to lock in cheaper rates when prices are falling. We pass savings through when we can. Our margin on Regular gasoline is often less than $0.15 per gallon.

We're not getting rich on this. We're trying to keep the pumps running and the lights on — same as everyone else.

Check live prices at amoco.fun before you drive over. And when you come in, treat the person behind the counter with the same respect you'd want for yourself.
